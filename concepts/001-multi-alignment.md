# 001 · Multi-alignment
*Filed 15 September 2026 — BRICS summit, New Delhi · revised same day, wire-sourced*

## Definition
A state deliberately maintaining working partnerships with rival powers at the same time,
refusing to convert any into an exclusive commitment, and treating that refusal as its
source of leverage rather than a problem to resolve.

## Lineage
Successor to **non-alignment** — and the distinction is the substance. Nehru's Non-Aligned
Movement (Bandung 1955, Belgrade 1961) was *abstention*: stay out of both Cold War blocs,
with an anti-colonial moral charge. Multi-alignment is *participation*: be in the Quad and
in BRICS, buy Russian crude and American aircraft, host Xi and sign with Washington.

Non-alignment said "neither." Multi-alignment says "both."

Key articulations: C. Raja Mohan's writing on India's strategic choices;
S. Jaishankar, *The India Way* (2020) — the standard reference text.

## Enabling conditions
1. **Weight** — each partner must want you more than it wants to punish you.
2. **Genuine rivalry among your partners** — each must fear driving you to the other.
3. **Domestic tolerance for apparent inconsistency** — the strategy looks like
   fence-sitting and must survive being called that.

India currently has all three. Turkey attempts the play with less weight. Most states
have none and must choose.

## Where it breaks
It is a bet against ever facing a forced choice. Cheap in a competitive multipolar world,
expensive in a hardening bipolar one. A Taiwan contingency is the standard hypothetical:
the space between poles compresses and the hedge stops paying.

## The realist critique
Multi-alignment accumulates customers, not allies. Transactional relationships do not
generate the obligation that matters in a crisis. When you finally need someone, you
discover you optimized for the wrong currency.

## The strongest objection
Not that multi-alignment is incoherent, but that it is **load-bearing for someone else**.

- Tanvi Madan (Brookings, to Reuters, 15 Sept 2026): convening adversaries builds a
  platform, and the platform's main beneficiary may not be the convenor — "bolstering a
  platform that enables China's global influence."
- Allen Carlson (Cornell): "If BRICS is a meeting of equals, China is the most equal."
  In a bloc of unequals the largest economy writes the agenda regardless of who hosts.

On this reading a middle power does not demonstrate leverage by convening; it supplies
venue, legitimacy and a photograph to a larger power's project, and pays the reputational
cost with its other partners for doing so.

**The reply.** This assumes influence is zero-sum between host and largest member. The
convenor's objective may be narrower and more defensible: keep a channel open to a rival
after a border conflict, keep a third power from becoming the rival's dependency, and keep
everyone aware that no relationship is exclusive. Carlson's own caveat — BRICS proposals
are "more show than substance" — cuts both ways: if nothing is built, the larger power got
a photograph too.

**What would settle it:** whether proposals made at such a venue acquire budgets and
institutions, and whose. Infrastructure is the tell.

**The older objection**, still worth keeping: that it is not a strategy at all — every
state manages multiple relationships, and calling it a doctrine dignifies ordinary
opportunism. The defense is that the refusal to choose is itself the strategic content:
not failing to pick a side, but investing in a world where sides remain pickable.

## Cases
- **BRICS Delhi, Sept 2026** — US trade deal (Feb) + hosting Xi and Putin (Sept) +
  a Gaza declaration Washington dislikes, with no cost paid yet.
- **Russian oil purchases post-2022** — India bought sanctioned crude at scale while
  deepening the Quad. The clearest demonstration and the clearest test.
- **Turkey** — S-400s from Russia while in NATO. Same logic, thinner base, real costs paid
  (F-35 expulsion). Illustrates condition 1.

## The open question
Strategy, or condition? Is multi-alignment a considered theory of order, or something
India can do only while the great powers are distracted — ending the moment they aren't?
