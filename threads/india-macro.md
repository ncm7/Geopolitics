# Thread: India's macro exposure to the Gulf
*Current as of 17 September 2026 · wire-sourced*

#### What moved — 17 September

The **Federal Reserve raised rates 25bp to 3.75–4.00%** on 16 September, unanimously — the first increase in three years. Chair **Kevin Warsh**: "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2% goal." August inflation ran 3.4%. Policymakers project one further quarter-point this year and none in 2027.

Energy: diesel set a record at **$6.396 a gallon**; Brent **$103.48** (−2.2%), WTI $100.65, against a pre-war crude level near $72. The Nifty 50 closed at a five-month low on 15 September on elevated oil and bond yields.

State the transmission chain plainly, because it is the whole thread: Hormuz → diesel → US CPI → the Fed → the global cost of capital → Indian equities and the rupee. India's rate problem is being set in Washington by an oil price set in the Gulf.

One political note. An administration with an implicit oil-under-$100 target now has a monetary authority acting on the same variable — which raises the price of Gulf escalation before 3 November and lowers the price of a settlement. Apply that frame to Trump's claim this week that Iran has approached him wanting a deal.

#### Why this is a geopolitics thread, not a markets one

India hosted a summit on global governance while a chokepoint 2,000km away set its interest rate. This thread exists to keep that link visible.

#### State of play

Brent sits at **$107.62** (LSEG, 16 Sept), down about 1% as Saudi Arabia offered more crude via Oman, with diesel near a record high. The Nifty 50 closed at a five-month low on 15 September on elevated oil and bond yields; Indian shares edged higher on the 16th with oil and US rate worries still capping gains. The US 10-year sits at 5.00% with a Fed decision due, and a rate hike is expected — a second, external squeeze on Indian assets.

One complication for yesterday's read: India's chief economic adviser says food inflation is **unlikely to persist through year-end** , and the August jobless rate fell to a six-month low of 5%. The rate-hike case rests on oil, not on the domestic basket. That makes it a foreign-policy problem wearing a monetary costume.

#### The strategic content

India imports the large majority of its crude, has no capacity to secure the Gulf militarily, and supplies a very large share of the world's merchant seafarers — leverage and exposure at once. Its instruments are commercial and diplomatic: discounted Russian barrels (now the target of the Graham Act), supplier diversification, and direct appeals to belligerents. Note what Saudi Arabia's Oman re-route means for Delhi: Asian refiners, India among them, are being offered crude outside Hormuz. Someone else's bypass is India's supply security this month.

#### What would count as real change

  * The rate hike landing, and whether it is framed domestically as an oil shock or a policy failure.
  * Any Indian move to secure shipping directly — escorts, insurance backstops, a naval deployment.
  * Graham Act tariffs actually imposed, which would hit the discount that has been absorbing part of this shock.
  * Hormuz risk premiums falling, which would take this thread dormant.
