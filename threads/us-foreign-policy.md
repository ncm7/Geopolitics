# Thread: US foreign policy and the 2026 midterms
*Current as of 16 September 2026 · wire-sourced*


Pending

#### State of play

Midterm elections 3 November 2026 — all 435 House seats, a third of the Senate. Trump is campaigning in North Carolina to rebuild 2024 support. The election becomes a geopolitical variable the moment it changes the coalition behind tariff policy, Ukraine aid, or Gulf escalation.

#### The live foreign-policy instruments

  * **The Graham Act** — now the sharpest of them, and the first that makes the election directly geopolitical: discretionary 100% tariff authority against buyers of Russian energy, handed to a president running a midterm.
  * **Oil under $100** — the administration's implicit target, and the reason the Ukraine energy truce is being pushed on diesel grounds.
  * **Naval escort in Hormuz** — an open-ended commitment with an election-sensitive price. The refusal to escort for Saudi Arabia should be read in this light.
  * **Munitions restocking** — Hegseth's ~$90bn request is a congressional fight with a five-year shadow over the Pacific.
  * **Ukraine aid** — the variable most directly exposed to the result.

#### The structural question

Every instrument above is coercive-economic or military, and each depends on the target having no alternatives. Whether US economic coercion still works in 2026 the way it worked in 2011 remains the single biggest open question in international political economy — and the Graham Act is about to test it on the largest available subject.
