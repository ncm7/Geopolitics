# Thread: Russia–Ukraine
*Current as of 17 September 2026 · wire-sourced*

#### What moved — 16–17 September

Rosneft's **Syzran and Saratov** refineries halted operations after Ukrainian drone attacks. On the running count, **three of Russia's six largest diesel-producing refineries** are now sharply reduced or fully shut. Global diesel set a record at $6.396 a gallon. The proposed energy truce remains unagreed and unobserved by both sides.

The Graham Act passed the House 262–159 and is on the president's desk — the Ukraine-adjacent instrument with the widest blast radius, since its tariff authority points at India and China rather than at Russia.


The energy war enters negotiation

#### State of play

Territory remains roughly static: Russia holds about 19–20% of Ukraine, around 45,000 square miles, with monthly gains in the low tens of square miles depending on whose count you take. Ukraine has launched an offensive push in northern Donetsk. Russian drones killed five on a bus and a train on 16 September and have struck within miles of Poland's border posts; an Italian NATO jet downed a suspected Russian drone over Lithuania. Casualty figures remain contested — roughly 1 million Russian killed and wounded and 250,000–300,000 Ukrainian, per Western official estimates compiled in early 2026. Treat every range as a range.

#### What moved: the deep-strike campaign became a bargaining chip

Trump called on Ukraine to stop hitting Russian diesel facilities, citing the global shortage. The Kremlin welcomed an energy truce and asked for sanctions relief. Zelenskiy said Ukraine would support one if Washington can vouch for Moscow's intent. Reuters reports both sides continuing energy strikes regardless.

The reason the campaign is on the table is that it works. Reuters' own calculation from fuel-market participants: **half of Russia's six largest diesel-producing refineries** cut back sharply or halted output in September because of drone damage. Global diesel is near a record high. Russia is meanwhile striking Ukraine's grid, more than 80% of whose generation capacity was destroyed or damaged as of August.

#### The structural point

Neither side is trying to win on the line of contact. Each is trying to break the other's economy — Russia against electricity, Ukraine against refining. An energy truce would therefore freeze not a front line but both theories of victory, which is why it is harder to agree than it sounds and why Moscow is willing to pay for it in sanctions relief.

#### The other lever

The Graham Act is in the House. EU envoys pushed back the expiry of Russia sanctions listings by seven days to 22 September after failing to agree a six-month renewal — worth watching as a measure of European cohesion under a US administration brokering.

#### What would count as real change

  * An energy ceasefire that actually holds for a fortnight.
  * Russian fuel scarcity reaching the domestic consumer economy.
  * Ukrainian grid collapse through a winter.
  * The EU failing to renew sanctions listings on 22 September.
  * A US aid decision after 3 November that changes Ukraine's material position.
