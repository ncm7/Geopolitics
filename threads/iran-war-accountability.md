# Thread: The Iran war's legal aftermath
*Opened 17 September 2026 · wire-sourced*

Cool

#### State of play

The independent fact-finding mission on Iran, established by the UN Human Rights Council in 2022 and chaired by the Bangladeshi jurist **Sara Hossain**, reported on 17 September.

**On the United States.** Reasonable grounds to believe that two strikes on **28 February 2026** constituted "launching indiscriminate attacks resulting in the loss of life or injury to civilians or damage to civilian objects" — a war crime.

  * **Minab** — the Shajareh Tayyebeh school. Iranian state media reported 168 deaths, mostly children; the mission concluded the school building "was the intended point of impact."
  * **Lamerd** — a sports complex and residential area struck with Precision Strike Missiles carrying tungsten pellets; 22 civilians reported killed. US Central Command denied responsibility.
  * At least 178 civilian deaths across the two. _Figures originate with Iranian state media and are carried rather than independently verified. Contested._

**On Iran.** Killings by state security forces on a "staggering scale," with torture, arbitrary detention and extrajudicial killings; more than 70 people identified at imminent risk of execution. Iran did not answer nearly three dozen inquiries.

The State Department did not immediately comment. Findings were to be presented to the 47-member Council.

#### Why it is structural

The mission has no enforcement power and will get none. Its product is a record, and records work on a decade timescale — raw material for later proceedings, for third states' justifications, and for the arguments belligerents eventually have with themselves.

The durable feature is the symmetry: one body, one report, findings against the intervening power **and** against the regime it fought. That is what will make the document hard to discard, and exactly why neither government will engage with it. Each can dismiss its own half by pointing at the other's.

#### What would count as real change

  * Release of the Pentagon's own investigation into either strike.
  * Any ICC or universal-jurisdiction proceeding citing the report.
  * A third state invoking the findings in a policy decision — arms transfers, basing, extradition.
  * Execution of the 70-plus identified, which would test whether naming protects anyone at all.
