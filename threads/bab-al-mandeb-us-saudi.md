# Thread: Bab al-Mandeb and the US–Saudi alliance
*Current as of 16 September 2026 · wire-sourced*


Active

#### State of play

The Houthis hold Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, the port of Mocha, and **Perim Island** in the Bab al-Mandeb itself, taken last week in an offensive that routed Saudi-backed forces. Fighting resumed in July 2026 when the Houthis declared a blockade on Saudi shipping in answer to a Saudi blockade of Yemeni ports. Saudi Arabia is striking Yemen — Mokha airport, Hodeidah — and calls an attempted strike near Mecca a "red line" (AP, 16 Sept); the Houthis deny that one. The UN counts more than 100,000 people displaced.

#### The development that changes the thread

US officials met Houthi representatives at the **US embassy in Muscat on Sunday 13 September** , five sources told Reuters, with Omani help. The Houthi side included Mohammad Abdulsalam, who is under US sanctions, and Abdelmalik al-Ajri. The Houthis said they remain committed to the May 2025 US ceasefire and will attack no American, Israeli or commercial vessels — **except Saudi ones**. The State Department would not confirm the meeting.

In the same week Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called Trump seeking US military support. Reuters reports Washington told Riyadh it would not get directly involved.

#### Why this is an alliance story, not only a Yemen story

Two facts sit together. The United States will negotiate with a proscribed organisation for the safety of its own shipping, and it will not fight for the shipping of a treaty-adjacent partner it has armed for fifty years. The Houthis have split freedom of navigation from the defence of Saudi Arabia, and American practice has accepted the split. Every Gulf capital is now recalculating what an American security guarantee covers.

#### Actors and incentives

  * **Houthis** — hold the chokepoint, and have discovered that selective exemption is worth more than closure. Exempting the US and Israel while targeting Saudi hulls isolates the opponent and keeps the hegemon out.
  * **Saudi Arabia** — striking Yemen without American help, with its Red Sea export route suspended and its bypass pipeline down. Its position is worse than at any point since 2015.
  * **United States** — wants freedom of navigation and oil under $100 before 3 November, and does not want a third theatre. An exemption is cheap; escort is not.
  * **Oman** — the mediator, and now also the physical bypass for Saudi crude loading off Sohar. Quietly the most useful state in the Gulf right now.
  * **Iran** — gets a Saudi adversary bled without a direct Iranian hand, and without triggering the US.

#### What would count as real change

  * Any US military action in support of Saudi Arabia — it would reverse the read above.
  * A Houthi attack on a non-Saudi commercial vessel, which would break the carve-out and prove it was tactical.
  * Saudi ground forces retaking coastline, or the Houthis taking Marib.
  * War-risk premiums for Bab al-Mandeb transits falling while the Houthis hold Perim — the test in today's concept.
  * A formal or public US–Houthi channel, which would be de facto recognition.
