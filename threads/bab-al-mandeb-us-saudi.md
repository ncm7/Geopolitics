# Thread: Bab al-Mandeb and the US–Saudi alliance
*Current as of 17 September 2026 · wire-sourced*

Active

#### State of play

The Houthis hold Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, the port of Mocha, and **Perim Island** in the Bab al-Mandeb itself. Fighting resumed in July 2026 when the Houthis declared a blockade on Saudi shipping in answer to a Saudi blockade of Yemeni ports. Saudi Arabia is striking Yemen and calls an intercepted drone near Mecca — roughly 680 miles from the Yemeni border — a "red line"; the Houthis deny that attack. The UN counts more than 100,000 people displaced.

#### What moved — 17 September

**Saudi Arabia has run out of road on interceptors.** AP, citing regional officials, reports that Riyadh has asked **France, Britain, Pakistan and Egypt** for air-defence assistance. One official said the United States, its principal supplier, is itself short after six months of war with Iran. Britain confirmed a "close defence partnership" without confirming a request. Turkey and Pakistan said no formal request had reached them. Vice President JD Vance said Washington is in "constant contact" with Riyadh and has held "direct conversations with the Houthis."

This revises the thread's founding reading. On 16 September it looked as though Washington _would not_ defend Saudi shipping. It now looks substantially as though it _cannot_ — a harder problem, because a decision can be reversed and a magazine cannot.

#### The Pakistan question

On 7 August 2026 Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey signed the **Mecca Joint Defence Agreement** — an attack on one is an attack on all, with a permanent secretariat in Saudi Arabia — building on the September 2025 Saudi–Pakistani Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement. Pakistan has around 8,000 troops and air force assets at King Abdulaziz Air Base and has joined the Saudi-led maritime alliance. It has not fired at the Houthis. Foreign ministry spokesman Sajjad Haider Khan, on operations against the group: "There is no discussion of this kind." Pakistan shares a 900km border with Iran, which is the reason.

For Delhi this cuts both ways. A Pakistan that will not fight for Saudi Arabia has a Gulf pact thinner than Indian analysts feared — and a Gulf that cannot buy security from anyone is a Gulf where Indian cargoes move on nobody's guarantee.

#### The development that opened this thread

US officials met Houthi representatives at the **US embassy in Muscat on Sunday 13 September**, five sources told Reuters, with Omani help. The Houthi side included Mohammad Abdulsalam, who is under US sanctions. The Houthis said they remain committed to the May 2025 US ceasefire and will attack no American, Israeli or commercial vessels — **except Saudi ones**. The State Department would not confirm the meeting. In the same week Mohammed bin Salman called Trump seeking US military support and was refused.

#### Why this is an alliance story, not only a Yemen story

An alliance is being revealed as a supply contract, and the supplier is out of stock. Extended deterrence rests on **inventory, not intent**, and inventory is now the binding constraint across two theatres at once. A collective-defence treaty six weeks old has produced troops, ships and communiqués but no shots. And look at whom Riyadh asked: Europe's two serious expeditionary powers plus two large Muslim armies. That is not a hedge against the United States. It is an attempt to reconstitute a guarantee out of spare parts.

#### Actors and incentives

  * **Houthis** — hold the chokepoint, and have found that selective exemption is worth more than closure.
  * **Saudi Arabia** — striking Yemen with a depleting magazine, Red Sea export route suspended, bypass pipeline down, four requests outstanding. Worst position since 2015.
  * **United States** — cannot resupply at the rate required and does not want a third theatre before 3 November. Offers diplomacy, including with the Houthis, in place of materiel.
  * **Pakistan** — treaty obligations on paper, an Iranian border in fact. Supplying presence, withholding force.
  * **Turkey** — sells the pact's defence equipment while mediating with Tehran.
  * **Oman** — mediator, and the physical bypass for Saudi crude loading off Sohar.
  * **Iran** — gets a Saudi adversary bled without a direct Iranian hand.

#### What would count as real change

  * Any of the four capitals publicly committing interceptors or crews.
  * A US emergency transfer — it would mean "would not" was the larger share after all.
  * Pakistani or Turkish forces engaging the Houthis: the first real test of the Mecca pact.
  * A Saudi purchase of a non-Western air-defence system, the clearest available signal of a patron change.
  * A Houthi attack on a non-Saudi commercial vessel, which would break the carve-out.
  * War-risk premiums for Bab al-Mandeb falling while the Houthis hold Perim.
