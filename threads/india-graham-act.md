# Thread: India and the Graham Act
*Current as of 17 September 2026 · wire-sourced*

Active — passed, on the desk

#### State of play

The **Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026** passed the House on **16 September, 262–159**, with 58 Democrats joining most Republicans over the objections of Democratic leadership. The Senate passed it 86–11 in August. **It is now on the president's desk.**

It sanctions Russian officials, companies and financial institutions; authorises tariffs of **up to 100%** on major importers of Russian oil and gas — China and India named — at the president's discretion to impose and to lift; and extends Iran sanctions through **2031**.

Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA), opposing: the bill "has a loophole that would allow him to define basically any country as a facilitator of evading Russian sanctions." That is the sharper objection, and it is not about Russia at all: a definitional loophole makes this a general-purpose coercive authority with a Russian title.

#### India's answer, 17 September

The Ministry of External Affairs: "India remains firmly committed to ensuring energy security for its 1.4 billion people. It will continue to do so through **diversified sourcing**." The bill's "potential implications for not just the bilateral relationship but also the international energy market have been very clearly articulated," and the government will work with Indian trade and industry bodies on the consequences.

No threat, no concession, no defence of the Russian barrel as such. The framing is _sourcing_ rather than alignment — an attempt to keep the purchase classified as commerce. That is multi-alignment's reply to a forced choice: deny that a choice is being offered, and keep buying.

#### Why the design matters more than the headline

A mandatory sanction is a policy. A discretionary tariff authority is a _lever_ : its value lies in being held rather than pulled, and it can be re-aimed at any moment without further legislation. Applied to a country that has bought heavily discounted Russian crude since 2022 while signing a trade deal with Washington in February 2026, it converts a standing commercial choice into a permanent negotiating instrument.

#### The link to concepts 001 and 003

Multi-alignment is a bet against ever facing a forced choice; this is the closest thing to one yet. And the act is the live case for **weaponized interdependence** (concept 003): a held instrument does not depreciate, which is why the discretion matters more than the 100% ceiling.

#### What would count as real change

  * Signature, and how quickly — a slow signature is itself a signal about intended use.
  * Any actual imposition on India, as opposed to the threat being held.
  * A measurable shift in Indian crude sourcing away from Russia.
  * An Indian counter-move — export controls, a WTO filing, or a quiet deal.
  * Use of the "facilitator" definition against a third country, which would confirm Beyer.
