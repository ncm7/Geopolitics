# Thread: India and the Graham Act
*Current as of 16 September 2026 · wire-sourced*


Live

#### State of play

The **Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026** passed the Senate 86–11 in August and is now before the House, with Trump's backing. It sanctions Russian officials and authorises tariffs of **up to 100%** on states deemed major consumers of Russian energy — China, India, Japan and some European allies — with the president free to impose and to lift them at discretion.

#### Why the design matters more than the headline

A mandatory sanction is a policy. A discretionary tariff authority is a _lever_ : its value lies in being held rather than pulled, and it can be re-aimed at any moment without further legislation. Applied to a country that has bought heavily discounted Russian crude since 2022 while signing a trade deal with Washington in February 2026, it converts a standing commercial choice into a permanent negotiating instrument.

Domestic resistance is real and economic rather than geopolitical. Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY): passing it as written "will not be sanctions on Russia or support for Ukraine, it will be higher costs and more inflation for the American people."

#### The link to concept 001

Multi-alignment is a bet against ever facing a forced choice. This is the closest thing to one yet — not a Taiwan contingency, but a bill that prices the Russian barrel in tariff points. Watch whether India responds by diversifying purchases quietly, by seeking an exemption, or by treating the threat as permanent background and doing nothing. The third would be the strongest evidence that the strategy is working.

#### What would count as real change

  * House passage, and whether the tariff authority survives intact.
  * Any actual imposition on India, as opposed to the threat being held.
  * A measurable shift in Indian crude sourcing away from Russia.
  * An Indian counter-move — export controls, a WTO filing, or a quiet deal.
