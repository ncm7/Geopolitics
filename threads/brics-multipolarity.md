# Thread: BRICS cohesion and the multipolarity question
*Current as of 16 September 2026 · wire-sourced*


Held

#### State of play

18th BRICS summit, New Delhi, 12–13 September 2026, hosted by Modi. The bloc adopted the **New Delhi Declaration** unanimously on day one: "deep concern" over the Middle East war, a call for "maximum restraint," a multilateral approach, peaceful resolution "through dialogue, consultation, and diplomacy," and objection to "unilateral tariff and non-tariff measures" that distort trade. Nearly 18,000 words. Attending: Xi (first India visit in seven years, after a border conflict), Putin, Pezeshkian, Indonesia's Prabowo, South Africa's Ramaphosa, Malaysia's Anwar Ibrahim. Brazil sent its foreign minister.

#### Membership — a live ambiguity

Reuters' own reporting lists ten members: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, UAE. Reuters elsewhere notes the bloc is "now comprising 11 countries, according to the official BRICS website." The discrepancy is real and usually traces to Saudi Arabia's unresolved status. Do not assert a number without saying whose count it is.

BRICS accounts for roughly 28% of global GDP. The concept originated with Jim O'Neill at Goldman Sachs a quarter-century ago as an investment thesis, not a political project — worth remembering when it is described as a bloc with a founding purpose.

#### The real cohesion constraint

Not India–China rivalry. **The bloc contains Iran and the United Arab Emirates simultaneously** , and the UAE is a Washington ally. Getting both behind one text on a Middle East war is the actual achievement; Reuters reports both backed it. Any analysis treating India–China as the binding constraint is using the wrong axis.

#### Whose agenda was it?

India hosted; China largely set the substance. Xi proposed a **BRICS AI Open Source Zone** that China would lead, a **BRICS Special Economic Zone partnership** , and more integrated supply chains.

  * **Tanvi Madan (Brookings):** the summit let India show "its diversified portfolio of partnerships" and be "a voice of the Global South and venue for adversaries to communicate" — but with tradeoffs "including bolstering a platform that enables China's global influence."
  * **Allen Carlson (Cornell):** "If BRICS is a meeting of equals, China is the most equal" — with the caveat that "BRICS proposals tend to be more show than substance."

#### De-dollarisation — corrected

Russia explicitly disclaims it. Peskov, 8 September: Russia "does not seek de-dollarisation" and is open to every acceptable payment method; some countries use their national currency as a political tool. Simultaneously: **90% of Russia–BRICS transactions are already in national currencies** , and Russian firms are accumulating rupees they cannot easily spend.

Read those together. Sanctions already did the displacing; Moscow prefers to attribute it to Washington rather than claim it as a project. The rupee overhang is what monetary autonomy actually costs, and it is why de-dollarisation stays largely rhetorical. India is separately pushing a BRICS digital currency link "despite hurdles."

#### Media environment

Ground News: BRICS coverage runs about **58% right-leaning sources** , dominated by Indian outlets — Times of India (Lean Right), Latestly (Right), Tribune India (Lean Right), Hindustan Times (Center), India Today (Lean Right). Expect the available copy to be friendlier to an Indian-triumph reading than the wires are.

#### What would count as real change

  * Any Xi proposal acquiring a budget and an institution. Infrastructure is the tell.
  * A BRICS settlement mechanism with real volume, not an MoU.
  * The US imposing the threatened tariffs on India — and India's response.
  * A China–India border incident during a period of BRICS cooperation.
  * Saudi Arabia resolving its membership status either way.
