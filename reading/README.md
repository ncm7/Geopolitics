# Reading

Books, essays and primary texts, with notes.

## Suggested starting points, given where the news currently is

**For multi-alignment and India's position**
- S. Jaishankar, *The India Way: Strategies for an Uncertain World* (2020) — the
  doctrine from the inside.
- C. Raja Mohan, *Crossing the Rubicon* — how India's post-1991 foreign policy was built.

**For the chokepoint and energy story**
- Daniel Yergin, *The Prize* — still the book on oil and power.
- Alfred Thayer Mahan, *The Influence of Sea Power Upon History* (1890) — the primary text
  everyone arguing about Hormuz is unknowingly quoting.

**For great-power competition**
- Thucydides, *History of the Peloponnesian War* — the Melian Dialogue especially. Read
  it before reading anyone who cites it.
- Hans Morgenthau, *Politics Among Nations* — classical realism, stated plainly.
- Henry Farrell & Abraham Newman, *Underground Empire* — weaponized interdependence,
  which is the concept behind every tariff threat in the news right now.

**For the philosophy underneath**
- Hobbes, *Leviathan* — Part I and II. The state of nature argument is the foundation of
  realism whether or not realists admit it.
- Machiavelli, *The Prince* and *Discourses* — read both; the second complicates the first.
